A Wedding at a 160-Year-Old Palace: Planning Guide

12 March 2026 · The Diggi Journal

Planning a wedding at a genuine heritage palace is a different exercise than booking a modern banquet hall — the venue comes with real architectural constraints, a working family history, and logistics that reward planning further in advance than a conventional venue would need.

Choosing the right space for your guest count

Heritage palaces built for royal hospitality often have several distinct venues within the same grounds, each suited to a different scale. At Diggi Palace, for example, the Durbar Hall accommodates up to 500 guests for a grand reception, the Green Sanctuary garden suits around 300 for a garden ceremony, and the more intimate Zenana Courtyard fits up to 80 — meaning a single property can often host the mehendi, ceremony and reception across three appropriately scaled spaces rather than one oversized hall.

What's different about a working heritage venue

Unlike a purpose-built event venue, a heritage palace is a lived-in, preserved structure — which means certain things (structural changes, some types of staging, pyrotechnics) are more restricted than at a modern venue, in order to protect the building. In exchange, you get architecture, gardens and atmosphere that no newly built venue can replicate.

Timing your wedding

October through March offers the most comfortable weather for outdoor ceremonies and garden receptions in Jaipur — the same window that draws the highest demand, so heritage venues book out furthest in advance for this period. If you're flexible, late monsoon (September) offers dramatic skies and lower rates, with a manageable risk of rain.

Working with a destination wedding team

A property that regularly hosts destination weddings — accommodation, catering, décor and cultural entertainment — will typically offer a single coordinated package rather than requiring you to source vendors independently. This matters especially for international couples managing logistics remotely; ask early about what's included versus what needs an external vendor.

What to ask before booking

Confirm exact guest capacities for each proposed space, ask what happens in case of rain for outdoor ceremonies, and clarify what decor and structural restrictions apply to a heritage building. A good venue team will have clear, direct answers to all three — vague answers are a sign to keep asking questions.

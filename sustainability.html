Three Hundred Years of Shade

What We Hold To

Not pledges for tomorrow — practices already in place. This is what homegrown means at our table, what centuries has taught these gardens, how rainwater finds its way back into the ground, and why composting never leaves the property.

Social responsibility

  • Hired from the lanes around us: most of the people who keep this house come from the neighbourhoods and villages closest to it.
  • Made by hands we know: linens from the block-printers of Bagru and Sanganer, tableware from Jaipur's own blue-pottery kilns — work kept with craftspeople a short drive from our gate.
  • Steady work, given near home: training and a fair day's place go first to those who live close by, so the house supports the streets around it.

Local heritage

  • Repaired, never replaced: what wears is mended in its own old materials — lime, stone and timber — rather than torn out for new.
  • Kept living, not framed: the palace is maintained as a place to be lived in, not a museum to be looked at.

Local culture

  • Courtyards meant for company: these verandahs were built to gather people, and we still lend them to music, conversation and the occasional festival.
  • Recipes kept in the family: the kitchen cooks Rajasthan's regional dishes from memory and habit, not from a manual.

Environmental protection

  • Water without plastic: copper jugs and earthen surahis cool the drinking water in every room, with no bottle to throw away.
  • Cooled by its own walls: thick lime walls, shaded verandahs and high latticed windows hold the heat at bay long before a fan is ever switched on.
  • Lit by the daytime: deep arches and open courtyards carry sunlight into the rooms, so little is lit until the evening.
  • Cloth, never paper: hand-block-printed cotton replaces paper and plastic — from the napkin at your table to the bag for your laundry.
  • In step with the season: the flowers in the vases and the fruit on the table follow whatever the month gives.

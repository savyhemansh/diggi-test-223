## 1. Executive Summary

The new Diggi Palace website is a **bespoke, hand-crafted digital property**. It was not assembled from a generic template or a drag-and-drop website builder. Every line of layout, every animation, every word of copy and every colour value was authored deliberately, in a professional code editor, by hand.

The result is a **17-page editorial website** that behaves less like a hotel brochure and more like a quiet, cinematic film about a 19th-century palace. It tells the story of Diggi Palace — *"hidden in the heart of Jaipur… a place where thoughts quieten, time softens, and every stay becomes a moment of renewal"* — through full-screen imagery, scroll-driven storytelling, restrained luxury typography, and an earthy heritage colour palette drawn from the building's own sandstone, brass and parchment.

At a glance, the build includes:

- **17 fully designed pages** — homepage, accommodation overview, four individual room pages, two restaurant pages, culinary, celebrations, events, curated experiences, history, sustainability, a gallery, a guest-voices page, and contact.
- **A complete in-house design system** — a single, disciplined visual language of colour, type and spacing that keeps all 17 pages feeling like one family.
- **Cinematic motion** — scroll-reveal animations, a pinned scroll-driven gallery, parallax imagery, and a full-screen elegant navigation menu, all written by hand for smoothness.
- **A professional SEO foundation** — structured data that tells Google exactly what Diggi Palace is, social-sharing cards, a sitemap, and Jaipur-specific local-search signals.
- **Enterprise-grade hosting** — served worldwide over a secure, encrypted, globally-distributed network with security headers in place.

This document explains all of it, and ends with a frank, unflinching list of what is still outstanding before the site is ready to take real bookings.

## 2. The Brief We Set Ourselves

Diggi Palace is not an ordinary hotel, and so an ordinary website would have been a quiet betrayal of the property. Before a single page was designed, the team studied the very best heritage and luxury-hospitality websites in the world — the rarefied tier of palace hotels, desert retreats and design-led sanctuaries whose digital presence is as considered as their architecture.

We did not copy them. We studied the **principles** that make those experiences feel expensive: the generosity of empty space, the patience of slow motion, the confidence to show one enormous photograph instead of ten small ones, the discipline of using only two typefaces. We then translated those principles into something that belongs entirely to Diggi Palace — its 1860 founding date, its courtyards, its greenery, its particular shade of warm sandstone.

The brief, in one sentence: **build a website that feels like a heritage palace — calm, timeless, confident, and unmistakably Indian — that a luxury traveller would trust with their honeymoon, their wedding, or their family's annual escape.**

## 3. Design Philosophy — The Soul of the Site

Luxury, on the web, is mostly about restraint. Cheaper websites shout: bright buttons, pop-ups, ten things blinking for attention. Expensive websites whisper. The entire Diggi Palace site is built on a philosophy of **calm confidence**, expressed through a handful of deliberate design principles:

**Negative space as luxury.** The most valuable material on a luxury page is empty space. Generous margins and unhurried spacing around every element tell the visitor, subconsciously, *"this brand is not desperate for your attention."* The site breathes. Headlines are given room. Images are given silence around them.

**Editorial, magazine-style layout.** Rather than the boxy, grid-locked feel of a booking site, the pages are composed like the spreads of a fine travel magazine — asymmetric, with text pinned to one side while imagery scrolls on the other, large pull-quotes, and a confident sense of rhythm down the page.

**One image, full screen.** The homepage opens not with a menu or a special offer, but with a single, silent, full-screen film of the palace. This is a deliberate emotional decision: before we ask the visitor for anything, we give them a feeling.

**Slow, cinematic motion.** Nothing on the site moves quickly. Elements fade and rise gently into view as you scroll. Images drift. The navigation menu unfolds in a graceful, staggered sequence. Speed feels cheap; slowness feels considered.

**Heritage, not nostalgia.** The palette and typography evoke history — parchment, brass, sandstone, a classical serif typeface — without feeling dusty or old-fashioned. The site feels like a 160-year-old palace that has been impeccably, contemporarily maintained.

**Gestalt and visual grouping.** Related information is grouped through proximity and shared spacing, so the eye understands the structure of a page before it reads a single word — a principle from classic visual-perception theory (the Gestalt laws of proximity and similarity).

**Hierarchy and the guided eye.** On every page, there is a clear first thing your eye lands on, a clear second, a clear third. This visual hierarchy — controlled through size, weight, colour and spacing — means the guest is gently guided rather than left to hunt.

## 4. The Design System — A Disciplined Language

One of the quiet achievements of this build is that all 17 pages feel like a single, coherent property — not 17 pages made by 17 different people. This is because the entire site is governed by a **design system**: a single, central rulebook of colour, typography and spacing that every page obeys.

In professional design, this is the difference between a hobby project and a brand. Think of it as the architectural code of a palace — the same sandstone, the same arches, the same proportions used in every wing, so the whole estate feels intentional.

The Diggi Palace design system defines:

- **A complete colour token system** — not just "brown" and "cream," but a precisely named hierarchy of more than 50 colour roles (primary, secondary, surface, container, outline, error, and so on). This is modelled on **Material Design 3**, the modern design framework developed by Google and used by some of the world's most disciplined digital products. It means colour is applied by *meaning* ("this is a primary action," "this is a quiet surface") rather than by guesswork.
- **A spacing system built on an 8-point grid.** Every margin, every gap, every padding value is a multiple of a base unit (8 pixels). This invisible mathematics is why the site feels so calm and orderly — the human eye registers consistent rhythm even when it can't name it.
- **A defined type scale** — a fixed set of text sizes for display headlines, headlines, body copy and labels, so that text never feels arbitrary.
- **Named brand tokens** unique to this property — including *aged parchment*, *gold shimmer*, and *midnight navy* — colours chosen specifically to evoke Diggi Palace's heritage character.

Because the system is centralised, a future change — say, shifting the brand gold slightly warmer — can be made in **one place** and ripple correctly across all 17 pages. That is a long-term gift to the property: the site is built to be maintained, not just launched.

## 5. Typography — The Voice of a Palace

If colour is the site's complexion, type is its voice. The Diggi Palace site speaks in **three carefully chosen typefaces**, each with a job:

**Didot — the display serif, for headlines and the wordmark.** Didot is one of the great aristocratic typefaces of European publishing — the face of high fashion magazines and luxury houses. Its dramatic contrast between hair-thin and thick strokes carries an unmistakable air of refinement. It is used for the "DIGGI PALACE" wordmark and every major heading. It is **self-hosted** — meaning the font files live on the property's own site rather than being borrowed from elsewhere — which protects both loading speed and brand consistency.

**Fraunces — the body serif, for paragraphs.** A warm, contemporary serif with a soft, slightly old-style character, used for all flowing body copy. Set at a generous line height (1.7), it gives long passages the unhurried, readable feel of a printed book rather than a screen.

**Inter — the humanist sans-serif, for labels and navigation.** A crisp, modern, highly legible typeface used for the small details: navigation, eyebrow labels, buttons, captions. These are set in capitals with wide, elegant letter-spacing — a classic luxury-editorial signature.

This **serif-for-soul, sans-for-signage** pairing is a deliberate, time-honoured combination. The contrast between the romantic serifs and the clean sans-serif labels is what gives the site its grown-up, magazine-grade polish. A full additional typeface family (Tiempos) is also bundled for editorial flexibility.

## 6. Colour — The Palette of Heritage

The colour story was drawn directly from the physical palace: warm sandstone walls, aged brass fittings, parchment, garden greenery, and the deep shadows of cool interiors.

The anchor colour is a **warm heritage brown (#705b3c)** — the colour of old sandstone and weathered brass — used for primary actions, the wordmark and key accents. Around it sits a family of soft, warm neutrals: an off-white *surface* (#faf9f6) that reads like aged paper rather than harsh white, layered "container" tones for subtle depth, and a near-black ink (#1a1c1a) for text that is softer and more refined than pure black.

This is **colour theory applied with intent.** The palette is almost entirely warm and low-saturation, which is precisely why the site feels peaceful and expensive rather than loud. A single restrained note of **gold shimmer (#D4AF37)** is held in reserve for the most special moments — a hairline divider in the footer, a delicate accent — so that when gold appears, it *means* something.

Crucially, colour is never decorative-only. Because the site uses a structured token system, text and background pairings were chosen with **contrast and legibility** in mind, so the romance of the palette never comes at the cost of readability.

## 7. Motion & Interaction — Cinema, Not Decoration

Animation on most websites is noise. On the Diggi Palace site, motion is used the way a good film uses a slow camera move: to create mood and to guide the eye. Every motion behaviour was written by hand and tuned by feel. Highlights include:

**Scroll-reveal.** As you move down any page, sections gently fade upward into view rather than snapping in. This is achieved using a modern browser capability that efficiently detects when an element enters the screen, so the effect is buttery and never janky.

**The pinned, scroll-driven gallery.** On the homepage, one column of text stays "pinned" in place while a series of large images scrolls past it — and the heading text changes in sync with whichever experience is on screen ("Private High Tea on the Lawns," "Meditation Mornings," "Royal Portrait Photo Shoots," and more). This is a sophisticated, premium interaction normally seen only on the world's most expensive hospitality sites. It was built entirely in-house.

**Horizontal storytelling strips.** The welcome story and the rooms section invite you to scroll *sideways* through a curated strip of imagery — a gallery-wall feeling that rewards exploration.

**The full-screen navigation menu.** Tapping the menu doesn't drop down a boring list — it unfolds an immersive full-screen experience: an elegant indexed list of the site (numbered 01–11) on one side, and a cinematic, subtly parallaxing image of the palace on the other, captioned "SINCE 1860." Menu items animate in one after another in a refined, staggered sequence.

**Micro-interactions.** Buttons depress very slightly when pressed. The navigation bar becomes subtly translucent as you scroll away from the top. Imagery drifts gently with the movement of the cursor. None of these are noticeable individually — and that is the point. Collectively, they are the difference between a site that feels *made* and one that feels *assembled*.

All of this motion is written with care to respect performance, so the cinematic feel does not come at the cost of speed.

## 9. The Words — Copywriting & Storytelling

A luxury website lives or dies on its words, and the copy here was written, not filled in. The tone throughout is hushed, evocative and confident. A few lines from the live site show the register:

> *"The Luxury of Stillness — hidden in the heart of Jaipur, Diggi Palace offers a rare sense of peace. Surrounded by nature and timeless beauty, it is a place where thoughts quieten, time softens, and every stay becomes a moment of renewal."*

## 10. How the Website Is Built — The Technology, Explained Simply

This section is for non-technical readers. The next section repeats the same information with full technical depth for anyone who wants it.

**It was hand-built in a professional code editor.** The entire website was authored in **Visual Studio Code**, the industry-standard professional code editor used by serious software teams worldwide. Nothing was generated by a website builder or a template store. Every page is original, hand-written code — which is precisely why it can look and behave like nothing else.

**It is built on the three core languages of the web.** Every website on earth is ultimately made of three things, and Diggi Palace uses all three the way they were meant to be used:
- **HTML** — the structure and content (the "bricks" of each page).
- **CSS** — the styling: colour, type, spacing, layout (the "interior design").
- **JavaScript** — the interactivity: the animations, the menu, the scroll effects (the "moving parts").

**It uses a professional styling framework.** On top of CSS, the site uses **Tailwind CSS**, a modern, widely-respected styling system used by many of the best product teams in the world. It allows for extremely precise, consistent design and keeps the final site lean and fast. On top of Tailwind sits the property's own custom design system described earlier.

**It is a "static" site — and that is a strength.** The site is built as a fast, static collection of pages. In plain terms: there is no slow, fragile database sitting between the guest and the page. When someone opens a page, it is delivered instantly and securely. Static sites are the fastest, most secure, and most reliable kind of website — exactly right for a brochure-and-booking property.

**It is delivered worldwide over a secure global network.** The site is hosted on **Vercel**, a premium, enterprise-grade hosting platform. Vercel stores copies of the site in data centres around the world, so a guest in Dubai and a guest in Delhi both receive it from a server near them, almost instantly, always over a secure encrypted connection.

## 11. The Engineering Detail — For the Technically Curious

For readers with a technical background, here is the full stack and architecture.

**Languages & markup.** Semantic **HTML5**, modern **CSS3**, and **vanilla JavaScript (ES6+)**. The JavaScript is deliberately framework-free — no React, no Vue, no jQuery dependency for the core experience — which keeps the payload minimal and the site fast and durable. All interactivity (scroll reveal, the menu overlay, the pinned gallery, carousels, parallax) is hand-written.

**Styling.** **Tailwind CSS v3.4** as the utility framework, compiled ahead of time into a single optimised stylesheet (`dist/tailwind.css`). The Tailwind configuration carries a fully customised theme: a 50+ token Material Design 3 colour system, a custom type scale, an 8px-based spacing scale, custom border-radius and font-family tokens, plus the official Tailwind **forms** and **container-queries** plugins. A hand-authored stylesheet (`css/custom.css`, ~520 lines) layers on the font-face declarations, typographic fine-tuning, scroll-reveal states and bespoke component styling.

**JavaScript architecture (`js/main.js`).** A single, well-commented global script orchestrates the experience:
- `IntersectionObserver`-driven scroll-reveal, used efficiently instead of expensive scroll-event listeners.
- A dynamically-injected full-screen menu overlay with staggered entrance animation, body-scroll locking, and a cursor-driven parallax on the menu image.
- A "pinned gallery" mechanic using CSS `sticky` positioning for the text column and `IntersectionObserver` to swap the active caption in sync with the imagery.
- Careful guard logic so micro-interaction transforms never fight with elements that rely on CSS transforms for positioning (a subtle, professional-grade detail).

**Fonts.** Self-hosted **Didot** (display) and **Tiempos** (editorial) families via `@font-face` with `font-display: swap`; **Fraunces** and **Inter** delivered via Google Fonts with `preconnect` resource hints; Material Symbols for iconography.

**Third-party components.** The contact page integrates **intl-tel-input v24.5** (via CDN) for a best-in-class international phone field with country auto-detection and a searchable 200+ country picker.

**Build & tooling.** Node/npm project; Tailwind compiled to a static CSS bundle. The deployed output is pure static HTML/CSS/JS — no server runtime required.

**Repository hygiene.** The project is version-controlled with **Git**, so every change is tracked and reversible.

## 12. Performance — Why the Site Feels Fast

Speed is a feature guests feel even if they never name it. A slow luxury site is a contradiction. Several deliberate engineering choices keep Diggi Palace fast:

- **Static delivery + global CDN.** Pages are pre-built and served from the nearest edge location, so there is no server "thinking time."
- **Aggressive, intelligent caching.** Images and fonts are cached for a full year as "immutable," while HTML is always revalidated so content updates appear immediately. CSS and JavaScript use a "stale-while-revalidate" strategy — guests get an instant cached copy while a fresh one quietly updates in the background.
- **Lazy-loaded imagery.** Off-screen images use `loading="lazy"` and `decoding="async"`, so the first screen appears fast and the rest loads as needed.
- **Resource hints.** `preconnect` to font servers shortens the time to first text.
- **Lean code.** No heavy front-end framework means there is very little code for the browser to download and run.

A formal performance audit (see Section 18) is one of the recommended next steps before launch, primarily to optimise image file sizes.

## 13. Search Engine Optimisation (SEO) — Being Found

A beautiful site that cannot be found is a private museum. Considerable foundational SEO work is already in place — and there is meaningful work still remaining. We will be completely candid about both.

**What is already done (and done well):**

- **Rich, hand-written metadata** on the homepage: a keyword-considered page title ("Diggi Palace | Luxury Heritage Hotel in Jaipur, Rajasthan"), a compelling meta description, and a canonical URL that prevents duplicate-content confusion.
- **Structured data (Schema.org `Hotel` JSON-LD).** This is the single most valuable SEO asset on the site. It is a machine-readable "identity card" that tells Google, in its own language, exactly what Diggi Palace is: a hotel, founded in 1860, at a specific Jaipur address, with specific coordinates, phone, email, star rating, price range, check-in/out times and amenities. This is what can make a property eligible for rich, prominent search results.
- **Full social-sharing optimisation.** Open Graph and Twitter Card tags mean that when the site is shared on WhatsApp, Facebook, LinkedIn or X, it appears as a beautiful, branded preview card with the right image and text — not a bare link.
- **Local-search signals.** Geo meta tags (region IN-RJ, Jaipur, latitude/longitude) help with "hotels in Jaipur"-style local discovery.
- **A complete XML sitemap** listing all major pages with priorities and update frequencies, plus a **robots.txt** that welcomes search engines and points them to the sitemap.
- **Crawl-friendly directives** (`index, follow`, large image previews enabled).

**What is still remaining (the honest list):**

- **Per-page metadata.** The homepage metadata is excellent; the remaining 16 pages need the same bespoke titles, descriptions and structured data tailored to each (rooms, dining, weddings, etc.). This is foundational and should be completed before launch.
- **Analytics & Search Console.** There is currently **no Google Analytics / Tag Manager and no Google Search Console** connection. Until these are installed, the property is flying blind — we cannot see traffic, search queries, or what guests do on the site. This is a top priority.
- **Google Business Profile.** SEO for a hotel is half on-site and half on Google Maps / Business Profile. That off-site presence must be claimed, verified and optimised, and connected to the site.
- **Image optimisation for SEO & speed.** The imagery is currently in large PNG format. Converting to modern formats (WebP/AVIF) and ensuring every image has descriptive, keyword-aware `alt` text will help both ranking and load speed.
- **Content depth & indexing.** Search engines reward genuine, regularly-updated content. A blog or "Journal" (Jaipur travel guides, wedding stories, seasonal notes) would meaningfully strengthen rankings over time. None exists yet.
- **Backlinks & off-page authority.** No off-page SEO (PR, listings, partnerships, directory presence) has been done. This is an ongoing marketing effort, not a one-time task.
- **A formal Lighthouse / Core Web Vitals audit** to confirm Google's performance, accessibility and SEO scores are in the green before launch.

In short: **the SEO foundation is professionally laid, but the house is not yet fully furnished.** The structured data and metadata work already done is the hard, technical part most agencies skip — but the analytics, per-page metadata, Business Profile and image work must be completed for the site to perform commercially.

## 14. Security — How the Site Is Protected

Security is where a "static" architecture quietly shines. Because the site has no live database and no traditional server processing guest input, the most common categories of website attack (database injection, server break-ins, plugin vulnerabilities) **simply have no door to knock on.** This is one of the strongest security postures a brochure-and-enquiry website can have.

On top of that architectural advantage, specific protections are in place:

- **HTTPS everywhere.** Every page is served over an encrypted connection, automatically, with certificates managed and renewed by the hosting platform. Guests' browsers show the secure padlock; nothing travels in plain text.
- **Security HTTP headers.** The site sends `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` (which stops browsers from being tricked into misreading files) and `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` (which protects guests' privacy as they navigate). These are configured at the hosting layer and apply to every page.
- **No secrets in the public site.** No passwords, keys or sensitive credentials are embedded in the public pages.
- **Reduced attack surface.** Minimal third-party code means fewer external points of risk. The site does not depend on a sprawling ecosystem of plugins that need constant patching.
- **Version control & reversibility.** Because every change is tracked in Git and every deployment is a discrete version, the site can be instantly rolled back to a known-good state if anything ever goes wrong.

**What remains on security (honest note):** the moment the site begins to *collect* guest data through working forms or take payments, the security requirements expand significantly — secure form handling, spam/bot protection, a privacy policy and cookie consent, and PCI-compliant payment handling will all be required. None of that is needed for the current brochure site, but all of it becomes mandatory at the booking stage (see Sections 18–19). We flag it now so there are no surprises later.

## 15. Hosting — How the Site Lives on the Internet

The website is hosted on **Vercel**, a modern, enterprise-grade hosting and delivery platform trusted by many of the world's leading digital brands. Here is how the pieces connect, in plain terms:

1. **The code lives in a version-controlled repository.** Every page, style and script is stored safely and historically (via Git).
2. **The hosting platform builds and distributes it.** When the site is published, the platform takes the code, prepares the optimised static files, and copies them to a worldwide network of data centres (a "CDN" — Content Delivery Network).
3. **Guests are served from the nearest location.** A visitor in Mumbai is served from an Indian edge; a visitor in New York from a US edge. Everyone gets a fast, local-feeling experience.
4. **The domain connects the brand to the build.** The property's domain (the `hoteldiggipalace.com` address referenced throughout the site) points to this hosting, so guests simply visit the brand's web address and receive the site.
5. **Smart routing rules are configured.** For example, visiting the old-style `/index.html` address permanently redirects to the clean homepage `/`, which is both tidier and better for SEO.

This separation — **code in one place, hosting in another, domain pointing to it** — is exactly how modern professional websites are structured. It is secure, scalable, and means the site can handle a sudden surge of traffic (after a press feature or a viral post) without buckling.

**What remains on hosting/connection (honest note):** final connection of the live domain and the property's official email (for example, ensuring `reservations@…` delivers correctly) should be verified end-to-end before public launch. The hosting and security scaffolding is ready; the final domain/email handshake is an operational step to confirm.

## 16. Accessibility & Responsiveness — Built for Every Guest

**Responsive on every device.** The site was designed mobile-aware throughout. Layouts reflow gracefully from large desktop monitors down to phones — the full-screen menu, for instance, becomes a stacked, touch-friendly experience on mobile, and imagery and type rescale appropriately. Given that the majority of hotel research now happens on phones, this is essential, not optional.

**Accessibility foundations.** The site uses semantic HTML structure, meaningful `alt` text on key imagery, keyboard-operable navigation, and high-contrast text pairings drawn from the colour system. 

**What remains (honest note):** a **formal accessibility audit against WCAG standards** has not yet been done, and should be — completing `alt` text on every image, verifying full keyboard and screen-reader operability, and adding ARIA labelling where interactions are custom. This both serves guests with disabilities and is increasingly a legal and reputational expectation for premium brands.

## 18. What Is Complete vs. What Remains — An Honest Accounting

We promised candour, and here it is in one place. This is the difference between a beautiful **presentation** of a hotel and a fully **operational** hotel website.

### ✅ Complete and strong
- 17 individually designed, on-brand pages.
- A full, centralised design system (colour, type, spacing).
- Cinematic, hand-built motion and interaction.
- Hand-written brand copywriting and storytelling.
- Homepage SEO: metadata, structured data, social cards, sitemap, robots, geo signals.
- Secure, fast, global static hosting with HTTPS and security headers.
- Responsive, mobile-aware layouts.
- International phone-enabled contact form (front-end).

### ⚠️ Remaining — important before public launch
- **Forms are not yet "live."** The contact and reservation forms are fully designed but **not connected to a backend** — submitting them does not yet send an email or store an enquiry anywhere. This is the single most important functional gap. Connecting them (to an email service or enquiry inbox, with spam protection) is essential.
- **No real booking/payment engine.** The reservation page is a visual prototype. There is **no connection to a booking system (PMS/CRS) and no payment gateway.** Taking real, paid bookings online is a separate, significant phase.
- **Imagery is placeholder-grade.** The site currently uses stand-in/mock imagery. It must be replaced with **professional photography and video of the actual Diggi Palace property** before launch — this is the highest-impact visual task remaining.
- **Per-page SEO + analytics + Google Business Profile** (see Section 13).
- **Image optimisation** to modern formats for speed and ranking.
- **Newsletter signup** is designed but not wired to an email marketing service.
- **No CMS (content management system).** Today, editing text or swapping a photo requires a developer. If the property's team wants to make edits themselves, a lightweight CMS layer is a worthwhile future addition.
- **Privacy policy, cookie consent, and terms** — legally required once analytics and forms are live.

1 · Executive Summary

The new Diggi Palace website is a bespoke, hand-crafted digital property. It was not assembled from a generic template or a drag-and-drop website builder. Every line of layout, every animation, every word of copy and every colour value was authored deliberately, in a professional code editor, by hand.

The result is a 17-page editorial website that behaves less like a hotel brochure and more like a quiet, cinematic film about a 19th-century palace. It tells the story of Diggi Palace — "hidden in the heart of Jaipur… a place where thoughts quieten, time softens, and every stay becomes a moment of renewal" — through full-screen imagery, scroll-driven storytelling, restrained luxury typography, and an earthy heritage palette drawn from the building's own sandstone, brass and parchment.

At a glance, the build includes:

  • 17 fully designed pages — homepage, accommodation overview, four individual room pages, two restaurant pages, culinary, celebrations, events, curated experiences, history, sustainability, a gallery, a guest-voices page, and contact.
  • A complete in-house design system — a single, disciplined visual language of colour, type and spacing that keeps all 17 pages feeling like one family.
  • Cinematic motion — scroll-reveal animations, a pinned scroll-driven gallery, parallax imagery and a full-screen elegant navigation menu, all written by hand.
  • A professional SEO foundation — structured data that tells Google exactly what Diggi Palace is, social-sharing cards, a sitemap and Jaipur-specific local-search signals.
  • Enterprise-grade hosting — served worldwide over a secure, encrypted, globally-distributed network with security headers in place.

This document explains all of it, and ends with a frank, unflinching list of what is still outstanding before the site is ready to take real bookings.

2 · The Brief We Set Ourselves

Diggi Palace is not an ordinary hotel, and so an ordinary website would have been a quiet betrayal of the property. Before a single page was designed, the team studied the very best heritage and luxury-hospitality websites in the world — the rarefied tier of palace hotels, desert retreats and design-led sanctuaries whose digital presence is as considered as their architecture.

We did not copy them. We studied the principles that make those experiences feel expensive: the generosity of empty space, the patience of slow motion, the confidence to show one enormous photograph instead of ten small ones, the discipline of using only two typefaces. We then translated those principles into something that belongs entirely to Diggi Palace — its 1860 founding date, its courtyards, its greenery, its particular shade of warm sandstone.

The brief, in one sentence: build a website that feels like a heritage palace — calm, timeless, confident and unmistakably Indian — that a luxury traveller would trust with their honeymoon, their wedding, or their family's annual escape.

3 · Design Philosophy — The Soul of the Site

Luxury, on the web, is mostly about restraint. Cheaper websites shout: bright buttons, pop-ups, ten things blinking for attention. Expensive websites whisper. The entire Diggi Palace site is built on a philosophy of calm confidence, expressed through a handful of deliberate design principles:

Negative space as luxury

The most valuable material on a luxury page is empty space. Generous margins and unhurried spacing tell the visitor, subconsciously, "this brand is not desperate for your attention." The site breathes.

Editorial, magazine-style layout

Rather than the boxy feel of a booking site, the pages are composed like spreads in a fine travel magazine — asymmetric, with text pinned to one side while imagery scrolls on the other, large pull-quotes and a confident rhythm down the page.

One image, full screen

The homepage opens not with a menu or a special offer, but with a single, silent, full-screen film of the palace. Before we ask the visitor for anything, we give them a feeling.

Slow, cinematic motion

Nothing moves quickly. Elements fade and rise gently as you scroll; the navigation unfolds in a graceful sequence. Speed feels cheap; slowness feels considered.

Heritage, not nostalgia

The palette and typography evoke history — parchment, brass, sandstone, a classical serif — without feeling dusty. The site feels like a 160-year-old palace, impeccably and contemporarily maintained.

Gestalt, hierarchy & the guided eye

Related information is grouped through proximity and shared spacing (the Gestalt laws of perception), and a clear visual hierarchy of size, weight and colour means the guest is gently guided rather than left to hunt.

4 · The Design System — A Disciplined Language

One quiet achievement of this build is that all 17 pages feel like a single, coherent property — not 17 pages made by 17 different people. This is because the entire site is governed by a design system: a single central rulebook of colour, typography and spacing that every page obeys. Think of it as the architectural code of a palace — the same sandstone, the same arches, the same proportions in every wing.

  • A complete colour token system — more than 50 precisely named colour roles (primary, secondary, surface, container, outline, error…), modelled on Material Design 3, the modern framework developed by Google. Colour is applied by meaning, not guesswork.
  • An 8-point spacing grid — every margin and gap is a multiple of 8 pixels. This invisible mathematics is why the site feels so calm and orderly.
  • A defined type scale — a fixed set of sizes for display, headline, body and label text.
  • Named brand tokens unique to this property — aged parchment, gold shimmer, midnight navy — chosen to evoke Diggi Palace's heritage character.

Because the system is centralised, a future change can be made in one place and ripple correctly across all 17 pages. The site is built to be maintained, not just launched.

5 · Typography — The Voice of a Palace

If colour is the site's complexion, type is its voice. The site speaks in three carefully chosen typefaces:

Didot — the display serif

One of the great aristocratic typefaces of European publishing — the face of high-fashion magazines and luxury houses. Its dramatic stroke contrast carries unmistakable refinement, used for the wordmark and every major heading. It is self-hosted — the font lives on the property's own site — protecting speed and brand consistency.

Fraunces — the body serif

A warm, contemporary serif used for all flowing copy. Set at a generous line height, it gives long passages the unhurried feel of a printed book.

Inter — the humanist sans-serif

A crisp, highly legible typeface for the small details: navigation, eyebrow labels, buttons, captions — set in capitals with wide, elegant letter-spacing, a classic luxury-editorial signature.

This serif-for-soul, sans-for-signage pairing is what gives the site its grown-up, magazine-grade polish. A further editorial family (Tiempos) is bundled for flexibility.

6 · Colour — The Palette of Heritage

The colour story was drawn directly from the physical palace: warm sandstone walls, aged brass, parchment, garden greenery and cool interior shadows.

The anchor is a warm heritage brown (#705b3c) — the colour of old sandstone and weathered brass — used for primary actions, the wordmark and key accents. Around it sits a family of soft, warm neutrals: an off-white surface (#faf9f6) that reads like aged paper rather than harsh white, layered "container" tones for depth, and a near-black ink (#1a1c1a) softer than pure black.

This is colour theory applied with intent. The palette is almost entirely warm and low-saturation, which is precisely why the site feels peaceful and expensive rather than loud. A single restrained note of gold shimmer (#D4AF37) is held in reserve for the most special moments — so that when gold appears, it means something. Because the site uses a structured token system, text/background pairings were chosen for contrast and legibility, so romance never costs readability.

7 · Motion & Interaction — Cinema, Not Decoration

Animation on most websites is noise. Here, motion is used the way a good film uses a slow camera move: to create mood and guide the eye. Every behaviour was written by hand and tuned by feel.

Scroll-reveal

Sections gently fade upward into view rather than snapping in — buttery and never janky, using an efficient modern browser capability.

The pinned, scroll-driven gallery

On the homepage, one column of text stays pinned while large images scroll past it — and the heading changes in sync ("Private High Tea on the Lawns," "Meditation Mornings," "Royal Portrait Photo Shoots"). A premium interaction normally seen only on the world's most expensive hospitality sites, built entirely in-house.

Horizontal storytelling strips

The welcome story and rooms section invite you to scroll sideways through curated imagery — a gallery-wall feeling that rewards exploration.

The full-screen navigation menu

Tapping the menu unfolds an immersive full-screen experience: an elegant indexed list (01–11) on one side and a cinematic, subtly parallaxing palace image captioned "SINCE 1860" on the other. Menu items animate in one after another in a refined, staggered sequence.

Micro-interactions

Buttons depress slightly when pressed; the navigation bar turns subtly translucent on scroll; imagery drifts gently with the cursor. None are noticeable individually — and that is the point. Collectively they are the difference between a site that feels made and one that feels assembled.

9 · The Words — Copywriting & Storytelling

A luxury website lives or dies on its words, and the copy here was written, not filled in. The tone is hushed, evocative and confident. Two lines from the live site show the register:

The Luxury of Stillness — hidden in the heart of Jaipur, Diggi Palace offers a rare sense of peace. Surrounded by nature and timeless beauty, it is a place where thoughts quieten, time softens, and every stay becomes a moment of renewal.
Beyond its timeless facade lies a collection of moments waiting to be discovered — sunlit courtyards, hidden pathways, quiet conversations, and memories that linger long after departure.

Notice what this copy does not do: it does not list amenities, shout about discounts, or mention Wi-Fi speeds. It sells a feeling — stillness, greenery, timelessness, renewal — which is exactly what a heritage guest is buying. The "Since 1860" motif recurs as a quiet badge of authenticity, and the experiences are named like chapters in a novel ("The Dawn Escape," "The Royal Chronicle"), not items on a menu.

10 · How the Website Is Built — Explained Simply

This section is for non-technical readers. The next repeats it with full technical depth.

Hand-built in a professional code editor

The entire website was authored in Visual Studio Code, the industry-standard professional code editor used by serious software teams worldwide. Nothing was generated by a website builder or template store. Every page is original, hand-written code — which is precisely why it can look and behave like nothing else.

Built on the three core languages of the web

Every website on earth is made of three things, and Diggi Palace uses all three as intended:

  • HTML — the structure and content (the "bricks").
  • CSS — the styling: colour, type, spacing, layout (the "interior design").
  • JavaScript — the interactivity: animations, the menu, scroll effects (the "moving parts").

A professional styling framework

On top of CSS, the site uses Tailwind CSS, a modern, widely-respected styling system used by many of the best product teams in the world. It enables precise, consistent design while keeping the site lean and fast. On top of Tailwind sits the property's own custom design system.

A "static" site — and that is a strength

The site is a fast, static collection of pages. In plain terms: there is no slow, fragile database between the guest and the page. Static sites are the fastest, most secure and most reliable kind of website — exactly right for a brochure-and-booking property.

Delivered worldwide over a secure network

The site is hosted on Vercel, a premium, enterprise-grade platform that stores copies of the site in data centres around the world, so every guest receives it from a server near them, almost instantly, always over a secure encrypted connection.

The JavaScript (js/main.js) is a single, well-commented global script: IntersectionObserver-driven scroll-reveal (used instead of expensive scroll listeners); a dynamically-injected full-screen menu overlay with staggered entrance, body-scroll locking and cursor parallax; a "pinned gallery" using CSS sticky for the text column and IntersectionObserver to swap captions in sync; and careful guard logic so micro-interaction transforms never fight elements that rely on CSS transforms for positioning — a subtle, professional-grade detail. The deployed output is pure static HTML/CSS/JS with no server runtime required.

12 · Performance — Why the Site Feels Fast

Speed is a feature guests feel even if they never name it. A slow luxury site is a contradiction. Several deliberate choices keep the site fast:

  • Static delivery + global CDN — no server "thinking time."
  • Intelligent caching — images and fonts cached for a year as "immutable"; HTML always revalidated so updates appear immediately; CSS/JS use "stale-while-revalidate" for instant loads with quiet background updates.
  • Lazy-loaded imagery — off-screen images load only as needed.
  • Resource hints — preconnect to font servers shortens time-to-first-text.
  • Lean code — no heavy framework means very little for the browser to download and run.

A formal performance audit is a recommended pre-launch step, primarily to optimise image file sizes.

13 · Search Engine Optimisation (SEO) — Being Found

A beautiful site that cannot be found is a private museum. Considerable foundational SEO work is in place — and meaningful work remains. We are candid about both.

What is already done (and done well)

  • Rich, hand-written metadata on the homepage: a keyword-considered title, a compelling description, and a canonical URL preventing duplicate-content confusion.
  • Structured data (Schema.org Hotel JSON-LD) — the single most valuable SEO asset on the site. A machine-readable "identity card" telling Google exactly what Diggi Palace is: a hotel, founded 1860, at a specific Jaipur address with coordinates, phone, email, star rating, price range, check-in/out times and amenities. This is what can make a property eligible for rich, prominent search results.
  • Full social-sharing optimisation — Open Graph & Twitter cards mean shares on WhatsApp, Facebook, LinkedIn or X appear as beautiful branded previews, not bare links.
  • Local-search signals — geo meta tags (region, city, coordinates) aid "hotels in Jaipur"-style discovery.
  • A complete XML sitemap with priorities and update frequencies, plus a robots.txt that welcomes search engines.

What is still remaining (the honest list)

  • Per-page metadata. The homepage is excellent; the other 16 pages need the same bespoke titles, descriptions and structured data. Foundational — should be done before launch.
  • Analytics & Search Console. There is currently no Google Analytics / Tag Manager and no Search Console connection. Until installed, the property is flying blind. Top priority.
  • Google Business Profile. Hotel SEO is half on-site and half on Google Maps. That presence must be claimed, verified, optimised and linked.
  • Image optimisation. Imagery is large PNGs; converting to WebP/AVIF and ensuring descriptive alt text helps both ranking and speed.
  • Content depth. A Journal/blog (Jaipur guides, wedding stories, seasonal notes) would strengthen rankings over time. None exists yet.
  • Backlinks & off-page authority. No PR, listings or directory presence yet — an ongoing effort, not a one-time task.
  • A formal Lighthouse / Core Web Vitals audit before launch.
In short: the SEO foundation is professionally laid, but the house is not yet fully furnished. The structured-data and metadata work already done is the hard, technical part most agencies skip — but the analytics, per-page metadata, Business Profile and image work must be completed for the site to perform commercially.

14 · Security — How the Site Is Protected

Security is where a "static" architecture quietly shines. Because the site has no live database and no traditional server processing guest input, the most common categories of attack (database injection, server break-ins, plugin vulnerabilities) simply have no door to knock on. This is one of the strongest security postures a brochure-and-enquiry website can have.

On top of that architectural advantage:

  • HTTPS everywhere — every page served over an encrypted connection, with certificates managed and renewed automatically. The secure padlock shows; nothing travels in plain text.
  • Security HTTP headers — X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff (stops browsers being tricked into misreading files) and Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin (protects guest privacy), applied to every page at the hosting layer.
  • No secrets in the public site — no passwords or keys embedded in the pages.
  • Reduced attack surface — minimal third-party code; no sprawling plugin ecosystem needing constant patching.
  • Version control & reversibility — every change is tracked in Git and every deployment is a discrete version, so the site can be instantly rolled back to a known-good state.
Honest note: the moment the site begins to collect guest data through working forms or take payments, requirements expand significantly — secure form handling, spam/bot protection, a privacy policy, cookie consent and PCI-compliant payment handling all become mandatory. None of that is needed for the current brochure site, but all of it becomes required at the booking stage. We flag it now so there are no surprises later.

15 · Hosting — How the Site Lives on the Internet

The website is hosted on Vercel, a modern, enterprise-grade hosting and delivery platform trusted by many of the world's leading digital brands. How the pieces connect, in plain terms:

  1. The code lives in a version-controlled repository — every page, style and script stored safely and historically (via Git).
  2. The platform builds and distributes it — it prepares the optimised static files and copies them to a worldwide network of data centres (a CDN).
  3. Guests are served from the nearest location — Mumbai from an Indian edge, New York from a US edge — fast and local-feeling for everyone.
  4. The domain connects brand to build — the property's web address points to this hosting, so guests simply visit the brand address and receive the site.
  5. Smart routing rules — e.g. the old /index.html address permanently redirects to the clean homepage, which is tidier and better for SEO.

This separation — code in one place, hosting in another, domain pointing to it — is exactly how modern professional websites are structured. It is secure, scalable, and can handle a sudden surge of traffic (after press or a viral post) without buckling.

Honest note: final connection of the live domain and the property's official email (ensuring reservations@… delivers correctly) should be verified end-to-end before public launch. The hosting and security scaffolding is ready; the final domain/email handshake is an operational step to confirm.

16 · Accessibility & Responsiveness — Built for Every Guest

Responsive on every device. The site was designed mobile-aware throughout. Layouts reflow gracefully from large desktop monitors down to phones — the full-screen menu becomes a stacked, touch-friendly experience on mobile, and imagery and type rescale appropriately. As most hotel research now happens on phones, this is essential.

Accessibility foundations. Semantic HTML structure, meaningful alt text on key imagery, keyboard-operable navigation, and high-contrast text pairings from the colour system.

Honest note: a formal accessibility audit against WCAG standards has not yet been done and should be — completing alt text on every image, verifying full keyboard and screen-reader operability, and adding ARIA labelling where interactions are custom. This serves guests with disabilities and is increasingly a legal and reputational expectation for premium brands.

17 · Quality, Testing & Browser Support

The site uses modern, broadly-supported web standards and degrades gracefully. It has been built and reviewed on contemporary browsers.

Remaining: a structured cross-browser and cross-device QA pass (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge; iOS and Android; a range of screen sizes) should be formally completed and logged before launch, along with the Lighthouse audit. This is standard pre-launch diligence and is not yet signed off.

18 · What Is Complete vs. What Remains — An Honest Accounting

We promised candour. This is the difference between a beautiful presentation of a hotel and a fully operational hotel website.

✓ Complete and strong

  • 17 individually designed, on-brand pages.
  • A full, centralised design system (colour, type, spacing).
  • Cinematic, hand-built motion and interaction.
  • Hand-written brand copywriting and storytelling.
  • Homepage SEO: metadata, structured data, social cards, sitemap, robots, geo signals.
  • Secure, fast, global static hosting with HTTPS and security headers.
  • Responsive, mobile-aware layouts.
  • International phone-enabled contact form (front-end).

⚠ Remaining — important before public launch

  • Forms are not yet "live." The contact and reservation forms are fully designed but not connected to a backend — submitting them does not yet send an email or store an enquiry. The single most important functional gap. Connecting them (to an email service or enquiry inbox, with spam protection) is essential.
  • No real booking/payment engine. The reservation page is a visual prototype. There is no connection to a booking system (PMS/CRS) and no payment gateway. Taking real, paid bookings is a separate, significant phase.
  • Imagery is placeholder-grade. The site uses stand-in/mock imagery. It must be replaced with professional photography and video of the actual Diggi Palace property before launch — the highest-impact visual task remaining.
  • Per-page SEO + analytics + Google Business Profile.
  • Image optimisation to modern formats for speed and ranking.
  • Newsletter signup is designed but not wired to an email service.
  • No CMS. Editing text or swapping a photo currently requires a developer. A lightweight CMS layer is a worthwhile future addition if the team wants to self-edit.
  • Privacy policy, cookie consent and terms — legally required once analytics and forms are live.
  • Formal accessibility (WCAG) audit and cross-browser QA sign-off.
  • Final domain & email connection verification.

None of these are signs of unfinished thinking — they are the natural, well-understood next phases of taking a site from exquisite brochure to commercial engine, listed here so management can plan with full visibility.

19 · The Roadmap — From Beautiful Brochure to Booking Engine

Phase 1 — Launch-ready

  1. Replace mock imagery with real property photography/video.
  2. Wire up the contact + newsletter forms to a real inbox / email service, with spam protection.
  3. Complete per-page SEO metadata and structured data.
  4. Install analytics + Search Console; claim & optimise Google Business Profile.
  5. Optimise images; run Lighthouse, accessibility and cross-browser QA.
  6. Add privacy policy & cookie consent. Verify domain + email end-to-end.

Phase 2 — Commercial

  1. Integrate a booking engine / channel manager and a secure payment gateway.
  2. Turn the reservation prototype into a real, transactional booking flow.

Phase 3 — Growth

  1. Add a Journal/blog for ongoing SEO and storytelling.
  2. Add a CMS so the property's team can self-manage content.
  3. Ongoing off-page SEO, content marketing and performance tuning.

20 · Closing Word

What has been built for Diggi Palace is not a website in the ordinary sense. It is a digital re-creation of a feeling — the stillness of a heritage courtyard, the warmth of old sandstone, the quiet confidence of a place that has stood since 1860. Every typeface, every shade of brown, every slow fade and every carefully written sentence was chosen to make a stranger across the world feel, for a moment, that they had already arrived.

The craft is real: a hand-coded, hand-designed, 17-page property with its own design language, cinematic motion, a professional SEO and security foundation, and enterprise hosting. The remaining work is equally real and has been laid out here without flattery — because a project worth this much care deserves honesty about what it still needs.

The foundation is exceptional. The path to launch is clear. And the destination — a Diggi Palace that is as unforgettable on a screen as it is in person — is well within reach.

Heritage Hotels in Jaipur: The Complete Guide (2026)

8 January 2026 · The Diggi Journal

Jaipur has more "heritage" hotels advertised than almost any city in India — but very few of them are actually classified as heritage properties, and fewer still are still owned and lived in by the families whose ancestors built them. If you're planning a Rajasthan trip and want the real thing rather than a themed lobby, this guide breaks down what to look for.

What actually counts as a heritage hotel

In India, "heritage hotel" isn't just marketing language — it's an official classification issued by the Ministry of Tourism, reserved for properties housed in genuine forts, palaces, havelis or castles that are at least several decades old and retain their original architecture. A heritage hotel has to preserve its structure rather than rebuild it, which is why these properties tend to have irregular room shapes, thick stone walls, and courtyards rather than corridors.

Diggi Palace, for instance, has been the ancestral town residence of the Diggi royal family of Rajasthan since 1860, and is classified as a heritage hotel by the Government of India — the palace still functions as a working family home alongside the hotel.

Palace, haveli, or fort: which is which

The three terms get used interchangeably by booking sites, but they describe different kinds of buildings. A palace was built as a royal or noble residence, usually with formal reception halls, gardens and separate wings for family and guests. A haveli is a large traditional townhouse, often merchant-built, centred on one or more internal courtyards. A fort hotel occupies a fortified structure built primarily for defence, later adapted for residence. We've written a full breakdown of the differences if you want to go deeper.

Where to stay in Jaipur, by neighbourhood

C-Scheme, where Diggi Palace is located, sits between the old walled city and the newer parts of Jaipur — close enough to Hawa Mahal and the City Palace for an easy morning walk, but quiet enough in the evenings that the palace gardens feel like a genuine retreat rather than a hotel car park. Properties inside the walled city put you steps from the bazaars but often mean narrower streets and more street noise; those further out toward Amer offer more space but longer drives to the main sights.

What to actually check before booking

Ask whether the property is still family-owned, how old the structure genuinely is (not just the brand), and whether the heritage classification is current. A good heritage hotel will be upfront about all three — and will usually have a story to tell, not just a rate card.

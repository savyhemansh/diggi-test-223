Rajasthani Thali Explained: What to Eat in Jaipur

19 February 2026 · The Diggi Journal

A Rajasthani thali is less a single dish than an entire regional cuisine served at once — a round platter of small bowls, each carrying a different flavour built to survive the desert climate the food comes from: rich, spiced, and often designed to keep without refrigeration.

Dal baati churma

The signature dish of Rajasthan. Baati are hard wheat rolls, traditionally baked over coals, cracked open and doused in ghee; dal is a lentil curry, usually a mix of several lentil varieties; churma is a sweet, crumbled wheat-and-jaggery accompaniment. Served together, the three form one of the most recognisable combinations in Indian regional food.

Laal maas

A fiery mutton curry coloured deep red by Mathania chillies, historically associated with Rajput royal kitchens and hunting traditions. It's one of the dishes most closely tied to Rajasthan's aristocratic food history, and a signature at heritage dining rooms across Jaipur, including Diggi Palace's Baradari restaurant.

Gatte ki sabzi

Gram-flour dumplings simmered in a spiced yogurt-based gravy — a dish that developed in Rajasthan's arid regions partly because it doesn't rely on fresh vegetables, which were historically harder to source consistently in the desert climate.

Ker sangri

A dry preparation of ker berries and sangri beans, both native to the Thar desert region — an example of Rajasthani cuisine's practical relationship with its landscape, turning drought-resistant desert plants into a distinctive, tangy side dish.

Where to try an authentic thali in Jaipur

Look for restaurants that serve thali as a full multi-course platter rather than a single curry with rice — the format is part of the experience. Baradari Restaurant at Diggi Palace, set within the historic pavilion for which it's named, serves a full royal Rajasthani thali in a heritage courtyard setting, and is one of the more traditional settings for the meal in central Jaipur.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is included in a traditional Rajasthani thali? expand_more

A traditional Rajasthani thali typically includes dal baati churma, laal maas or another curry, gatte ki sabzi, ker sangri, rice, roti and a sweet — served as a full platter of small dishes rather than a single course.
